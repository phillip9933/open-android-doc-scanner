# OpenCV image processing

`dev.offlinescan.processing.OpenCvProcessor()` implements the platform-free `ImageProcessor` contract. Execute its synchronous methods on a background thread. `detectBitmap` borrows the camera bitmap and returns normalized corners in that bitmap's visual orientation; it never recycles the caller's bitmap. File methods apply all eight EXIF orientation transforms before interpreting corners. The bundled OpenCV library loads through `OpenCVLoader.initLocal()` once, without a manager app, network service, or runtime download.

Original and Photo keep RGB color and apply no automatic tonal change. Color Document adds only 4% contrast. Grayscale and Black White are explicit choices. User brightness is an additive fraction of 255; contrast pivots around 128. Illumination adds a restrained, spatially smooth luminance offset equally to RGB channels, preserving color rather than whitening the image. Black White uses local Gaussian adaptive thresholding. Rotation applies after perspective cropping; edits are nondestructive and the original file cannot be overwritten.

Before decoding, image bounds are checked against `ScanConfig.maxInputPixels`, a 100,000-pixel dimension limit, and a 128 MB encoded-file limit. Detection samples to a longest edge of at most 960 pixels. Rendering samples to at most 4096 pixels on the longest edge and 6 million pixels total. A perspective output is bounded by the configured maximum dimension and 8 million pixels, and is never intentionally upscaled beyond the measured crop edges. This may yield fewer pixels than a requested 8192-pixel maximum. Reported dimensions describe the actual encoded output after rotation. Crop points must form a finite clockwise convex quadrilateral; crops with extremely short edges or nearly collinear corners are rejected before the warp. Android and native temporary image allocations are released in `finally`/scope cleanup, including cancellation. Cancellation exceptions propagate to the caller.

Detection operates on bounded grayscale. Strong Canny edges form the first pass, weaker edges recover faint boundaries, and Otsu segmentation is a last resort. Small morphological closing connects edge gaps; segmentation uses a larger kernel to bridge thin ink cuts. Adaptive polygon approximation proposes convex quadrilaterals from contours and their hulls. A hull is only a proposal: straight sides must be supported by the original contour, and inside/outside samples must demonstrate contrast along all four sides. Fitted side-line intersections refine actual corners rather than selecting a bounding rectangle. Clipped boundaries, weak side support, low solidity and nearly degenerate geometry are rejected. Candidates cover at least 1.5% and less than 96% of the frame, with sides at least 18 detection pixels long. Strong supported candidates skip additional passes; each pass evaluates at most 120 contours after area filtering.

If those passes find no supported candidate, two additional passes use a candidate-only illumination correction: a coarse smooth luminance field divides the original grayscale with bounded amplification, followed by edge detection and segmentation. Existing accepted candidates return before this correction. Corrected-image contrast alone is insufficient: each proposed side also needs a measured jump in the untouched image after subtracting the local illumination slope. This helps distinguish faint paper boundaries from a broad penumbra. Corrected candidates retain all convexity, contour-support and clipping checks, and confidence uses original edge strength with a lower 0.86 cap. Raw brightness, sharpness, scene signature and rendered pixels remain unchanged. Worst-case analysis can run five passes; difficult negative scenes cost more time.

It reports a heuristic confidence score capped at 0.94, whole-frame brightness from 0 to 1, raw grayscale Laplacian variance for sharpness, and 64 normalized grayscale region means in row-major 8-by-8 order as the scene signature. Confidence incorporates observed side contrast/coverage and geometry; it is not a calibrated probability. A missing candidate returns null corners and zero confidence; image boundaries and inferred boxes are never fabricated as confident documents. Automatic capture must combine confidence with stability, brightness, focus and repeated-frame evidence. Displayable small receipts can still fall below the separate capture gate's area threshold.

Limitations: detection cannot identify document semantics and may select a rectangular screen, frame, or tabletop. Low contrast, glare, complex backgrounds, curved pages, partly obscured borders, very thin receipts, extreme perspective, and a page filling nearly the entire frame can prevent detection. Manual corner adjustment remains necessary. This algorithm does not flatten book curvature, restore missing detail, infer physical paper aspect ratios, perform OCR, remove glare, or perform photographic white-balance correction. Sharpness thresholds vary with camera resolution and lighting. Wide color gamut/HDR input is decoded to standard Android ARGB/RGB output; originals remain available. Cancellation is checked between native operations and periodically during illumination correction, but cannot interrupt a native OpenCV call or Android image encoder already running.

Instrumentation tests generate bounded synthetic fixtures for empty scenes, perspective and rotated documents, every EXIF orientation, color preservation, grayscale/BW selection, dimension bounds, invalid geometry, input limits, and cancellation. The original benchmark remains unchanged. A separate challenge cohort records faint/light backgrounds, broad shadows, narrow receipts, clutter, perspective, soft edges, nonrectangular negatives and clipped pages. Its tuning fixtures informed this revision; its held-out variants remain separate evaluation evidence. Additional checks enforce tuning corner quality and live/still parity, and sample repeated detection latency without claiming peak memory or physical-device thermal performance. Run `:scanner-processing-opencv:connectedDebugAndroidTest` on an Android API 26+ device or emulator with an OpenCV-supported ABI. Synthetic tests establish invariants; real camera validation remains essential.

A further independent temporal-shadow cohort applies small camera translations/rotations to complete scenes with light/overlapping/moving shadows, textured light backgrounds and negative scenes. Each frame has independent truth. Measurements report recall, IoU including misses, lost-outline transitions and truth-compensated corner jitter with the number of matched adjacent pairs. Missing frames are counted separately; they cannot improve apparent jitter by silently disappearing. These synthetic transforms use zero-filled overscan at the outer image border, which can affect whole-frame sharpness; this cohort does not establish automatic-capture focus acceptance. Cancellation during the corrected fallback is also tested. Real handheld footage and physical-device measurements remain necessary.
