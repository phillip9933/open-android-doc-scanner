# Document quality sampling for RC8

The Standard detector and the learned-detector metric adapter now report untouched document-interior sharpness and brightness when they have valid corners. A textured table, a contrasting paper border, or light outside the document no longer supplies focus evidence for the document. Corner finding and proposal confidence are unchanged by this quality work.

`OpenCvProcessor.assessQuality(source, corners, config, cancellation)` reads supplied corners in the upright EXIF orientation and bounds decoding to 960 pixels and 921,600 decoded pixels. `assessQualityBitmap(bitmap, corners, cancellation)` retains caller bitmap ownership. Both return `DocumentQuality(sharpness, brightness, informativeTiles, tileCount)` through the same metric. `measureDetectionBitmap(bitmap, corners, confidence, cancellation)` attaches these metrics and the unchanged 8-by-8 scene signature to corners supplied by an independent detector without running Standard corner detection.

The document is rectified to a 512-pixel long edge at its observed aspect ratio. An eight-percent margin excludes outer edges. Nine interior tiles contribute Laplacian variance with their one-pixel computational borders removed. The sharpness score is the upper quartile of informative tile scores: blank margins do not dilute sparse printed content. A tile is informative only when grayscale standard deviation is at least 8 and mean absolute residual from a Gaussian illumination field (sigma 10) is at least 3. Smooth shadows should not count as text. Brightness is the mean of the same nine tiles. When no corners are found, Detection keeps legacy whole-frame brightness and sharpness only for tracking a temporary detection miss; documentDetailTiles is null.

Uniform paper deliberately has zero informative tiles and nearly zero sharpness. Low sharpness alone cannot establish that a blank document is blurry. Camera policy must use the reported detail count and focus/stability evidence. If detailed preview content disappears in the captured still, it must not be treated as a newly blank document.

The metric is a bounded relative focus measure, not a guarantee of readable text. It does not undo motion blur, fuse exposures, assess clipped highlights, or prove focus on blank paper. Strong internal creases, printed borders inside the margin, sensor noise, and dense photographic texture can still add energy. An upper-quartile score can miss localized blur in other tiles. Severe perspective and documents occupying few source pixels lose detail before rectification; upsampling cannot restore it. File decode sampling and live bitmap resizing may differ slightly before the common rectification, so a live/still ratio needs conservative interpretation. It measures approximately 512-pixel document detail rather than full-resolution output MTF.

`DocumentQualityTest` covers sharply textured backgrounds around locally blurred document content in frontal and angled views; moderate blur of low-contrast ink; blank paper with high-contrast surrounding edges and smooth shading; lossless bitmap/file parity; upright EXIF rotation parity; and cancellation plus caller ownership. These synthetic cases support threshold review but do not replace physical phone testing across document sizes, lighting, receipts, autofocus behavior, and hand movement.

All five quality tests and eight existing processor tests passed on the owned API 35 Android emulator. Frontal print measured sharpness 2,893.81 versus 6.67 after document-only Gaussian sigma-4 blur. Angled print measured 2,237.55 versus 7.66. Low-contrast angled print (gray 180 ink on gray 238 paper) measured 166.25 versus 11.10 after Gaussian sigma-1.5 blur; the blurred/sharp ratio was 0.0668. All six printed cases retained nine informative tiles. The sharply textured background therefore did not make the blurred page meet the existing minimum of 60. Uniform paper and smooth-shaded paper remained uninformative; bitmap/file and upright EXIF metrics matched within test tolerances.

## Automatic capture integration evidence

Three `ScannerAutomaticCaptureTest` tests passed on the owned API 35 emulator in 13.652 seconds. They use the actual virtualscene camera, CameraX focus/capture, encoded JPEG files, and the real quality processor. Only document corners (the full shared viewport) and a common scene signature are supplied by the test, to isolate capture and quality behavior from corner-detector accuracy. Sharpness, detail count, brightness, camera stills, and lifecycle events are not mocked.

- Successful automatic capture assesses exactly two encoded stills. The delivered JPEG's SHA-256 equals the sharpest candidate's original SHA-256, proving selection without recompression. It decodes successfully, and no temporary `.scan-` files remain.
- Returning no corners for both stills rejects the burst exactly once, with no delivered file or temporary residue. A subsequent manual capture succeeds, demonstrating that rejection releases the capture gate.
- Stopping the activity during an automatic capture rejects it once and leaves no output. After lifecycle resume and fresh analysis, a manual capture succeeds. The old capture does not deliver an image or a second rejection later.

This evidence validates original selection, quality processing, cleanup, and recovery in the controlled emulator pipeline. It does not establish physical-camera autofocus success, readable text at full output resolution, document detection recall, true scene-match discrimination, or quality under real handheld motion and exposure changes. The supplied corners and signature deliberately bypass those detector decisions. Blank documents still require focus/stability policy because their pixels provide insufficient sharpness evidence. Choosing the best of two originals does not fuse images or guarantee that either candidate is sharp enough on every phone.
